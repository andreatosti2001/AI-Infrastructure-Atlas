**Reader:** a fresh agent (not an agent role; Part B §10), given only `reader-input/` (screenshots at 1280 and
375 px and the visible text of the page with its tutorials removed) and the form. It wrote nothing to the
repository. Answered on 2026-10-07, before any S16 finding was written. Copied as given.

1. Evidence coverage: The Atlas seems to record four links for each accelerator: who acts on it, that it incorporates HBM, who makes the HBM, and that HBM requires 3D stacking. Each link is graded stated, inferred or gap. Trainium2 has 2 stated, 1 inferred and 1 gap. H100 has 1 stated, 1 inferred and 2 gaps. So on paper Trainium2 is a little better documented, though with only four links each the difference is one link. I don't follow "rows drawn from 3 records, of which shared with another accelerator: 1": four links come from three records, and I can only guess that the shared one is the HBM-requires-stacking record. One thing puzzled me. H100's "TSMC fabricates" link is a gap because its evidence is "not fresh" (2025-07-22). Yet Trainium2's "Amazon designs" link is stated even though its evidence is older (2023-11-28). That makes me unsure what "stated" versus "gap" actually measures.

2. Evidence age as of 2026-10-06: Out of the 3 dated links for each accelerator (the supplier gap has no date), the big number counts how many have newest evidence older than twelve months. That is 2 of 3 for Trainium2 and 3 of 3 for H100, so a higher number is worse, which is easy to misread as a score. Trainium2's one "recent" link is dated only by the day a web page was read (2026-10-01), not by a publication date. My conclusion is that nearly all the evidence is more than a year old, and Trainium2 looks fresher mainly because a page was looked at recently. The heading says "Evidence age" but the note says "Dates, not ages", which confused me slightly.

3. Publishers behind a sourced link: I read "1 to 2" as each sourced link resting on one or two publishers. Of 6 sourced rows (the 8 rows minus the 2 unsourced supplier gaps), 4 rest on a single publisher, and judging by the table that publisher is the company itself (Amazon/AWS or NVIDIA). The 2 two-publisher rows are the same stacking link counted once per accelerator, so it is really one link. I take "Listed, never counted as confirmation" to mean that having two publishers is shown but not treated as independent corroboration, though the wording is terse. My conclusion is that there is very little independent confirmation: most claims are vendors describing their own products.

4. Supplier count (cannot be computed yet): Nobody has researched who makes the HBM in either accelerator (2 of 2 not researched), so the number of suppliers can't be counted. It needs a source naming each supplier. I would conclude that the page cannot yet answer the "who makes it" half of its own headline question. This is unknown, not zero suppliers.

5. Geographic concentration (cannot be computed yet): This depends on first knowing the suppliers and then where each one makes the part, and neither is known. I can conclude nothing about where HBM production is located or how concentrated it is.

6. Single-source relationships (cannot be computed yet): This would need a source stating that a part has exactly one supplier. Since suppliers aren't researched at all, there is no evidence either way. That is not a sign of single-sourcing or multi-sourcing. The three blocks repeat the same middle line word for word, so only the "needs" line tells them apart.

7. The indicators together: Both accelerators are stated to use HBM, and HBM is inferred, from 2018–2020 evidence, to need 3D stacking. Trainium2 has one more stated link and one fresher date, but that date is only a page-read date. To me the differences reflect how far the Atlas's research has got, not real differences between the chips. I would not conclude that either accelerator is more or less dependent or exposed: the decisive facts (who makes the memory, where, and whether there's only one source) are all missing. The unexplained stated/gap treatment of the Amazon and TSMC links also makes me cautious about reading anything into the coverage difference.
