Atlas framing

This brief answers one question from the Atlas’s records, over the scope stated below. Every value in it is generated from a record or a computed result by a tested build step and opens its evidence; the Atlas’s own explanations are marked as framing, like this box.

Atlas framing

The scope is what the brief reads. The chain is the dependency result: each link the Atlas records from an accelerator to the memory inside it and to the process that memory needs, with who designs, fabricates or supplies each part. The policy layer is every export-control text the Atlas records, compared with the classes that chain reaches.

  • Whether any product, firm or transaction falls under a text: the Atlas never makes that determination.
  • The law of any other jurisdiction.
  • Market share, price, volume, capacity or customers.

Each sentence above groups rows of the dependency result or of the policy records and states them with their basis: stated by a party, reported by a third party, inferred by the Atlas, or a recorded gap with its reason. A count counts the Atlas’s records, never the world. A class with no recorded relationship to a text is unknown to the Atlas, not outside the text.

Atlas framing

Each row is a class the chain reaches; each column is a recorded act. “Named in the text” means the act’s own words name the class among the items it controls, as quoted in the evidence. “Refused” gives the reason the Atlas recorded for not linking the pair, for example a term in the text that is broader than the class. The Atlas never compares a product with a text: whether a product meets a text’s parameters is a legal determination.

Atlas framing

A status is derived on the as-of date from the act’s dated events, by the Atlas’s status rule. “Has taken effect” means that an effective date recorded for the act lies before that date and that no repeal is recorded in the Atlas; it never says that the act applies to anyone. “No recorded effect” means that the Atlas holds no effective date for the act: unknown, not “not in effect”.

Atlas framing

The brief is generated from the Atlas’s data files and its computed results by a tested build step; nothing on it is typed by hand. Only canonical records appear: records awaiting review are left out. The summary copies values, counts the rows it shows, takes the first and last of a date column, and groups rows; it computes nothing else. A status comes from the Atlas’s status rule on the as-of date. The digest identifies the exact inputs; rebuilding from the same inputs gives the same brief, byte for byte.
