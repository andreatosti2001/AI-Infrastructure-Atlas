Statuses

What a status means

Atlas framing

A status is derived on the as-of date from the act’s dated events, by the Atlas’s status rule. “Has taken effect” means that an effective date recorded for the act lies before that date and that no repeal is recorded in the Atlas; it never says that the act applies to anyone. “No recorded effect” means that the Atlas holds no effective date for the act: unknown, not “not in effect”. A later act that changes an entry is quoted in the evidence of the link it concerns; the Atlas never infers an end date from it.

The rule's word is the record's alias HBM, applied to stacks. 'With a specific memory bandwidth density' limits which stacks the entry covers; that is scope, which stays in the text, not a different class. The rule also defines its term at its edges (p.11: high bandwidth memory includes DRAM ICs whether or not they conform to the JEDEC standards, above its density threshold; co-packaged logic and HBM chips are not covered by 3A090.c), so the text's class and the Atlas's may differ at their edges; the link does not carry either edge. It concludes only that the two words name the same kind of part. It does not conclude that every member of the class, or any product or firm, is controlled, for which destination or under which licence policy, or that the control is in effect on any date (policy-links.md K-2, D-167).

Terms

Relation types

restricts
The source act's own text names the target class among the items it controls (S18; policy-links.md). Never that every member of the class, or any product or firm, is controlled; never for which destination or under which licence policy; never that the control is in effect today.

Claim types

ATTRIBUTION
What a named source says, defines, claims, plans or estimates, recorded as that source's statement. Superlatives, intentions and second-hand reports are always this type (ER-5, ER-6, ER-8).
DERIVATION
A proposition that follows from other claims, or from a recorded search, by a stated step. The Atlas's inference, shown as inferred.
FACT
A proposition about the world, stated by a first-hand source (standing party or originator) at its locator. Never the Atlas's own inference.
INTERPRETATION
The Atlas's own judgement or choice, such as a working definition, citing the claims it interprets.

Evidence status

supported
The Verifier read each cited locator, found the anchor, and judged that the sentence states the proposition and the wording does not exceed it; for a claim with inputs, every input is supported and the stated step follows. MA §10 'direct', renamed.

Standing

originator
source-policy.md §4, row originator.

source-policy.md §4, row party.

Unknown and unstated values

not stated
The source gives no as-of date for this content.
undated
The source states no date of any kind (register: 'undated (live page)'). Its access date is its only temporal anchor.

Source classes

government publication
A publication or record issued by a government agency, programme office, regulator or intergovernmental organisation in its official capacity: definitions, programme documents, official registers, permits, statistics.
official legal text
The text of a law, regulation or rule as an official journal or official code publishes it, by or for the body that issues it (e.g. the Federal Register and the Code of Federal Regulations on govinfo.gov). It is the act itself, not a statement about it: a press release or fact sheet about an act is a government_publication (S17; source-policy.md §2, D-158).
policy research brief
Analysis framed to inform policy decisions, published by a policy research centre or think tank (e.g. an issue brief addressed to policymakers), whether or not the centre sits in a university.
research report
A report of research methods and results by a research organisation (national laboratory, university, independent research institute), including peer-reviewed papers, even when a government sponsors the work.
