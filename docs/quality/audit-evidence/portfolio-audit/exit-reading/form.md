# The exit-condition reading: the form

S25, 2026-10-16 (D-228). The roadmap's condition for v1.0 is that another person can understand seven things about
the Atlas. Two fresh readers who had never seen the project each answered this form before anyone assessed their
answers. Their answers are kept as given in this folder, with the reader's brief and the hash of the tree they read
(`input.md`).

## The reader's instructions

You are reading a project for the first time. You have one folder: the files of its release candidate, exactly as
they will be published. Start with `README.md`; open any file in the folder that helps you, the generated pages in
`site/` included. Do not use the web, Git history or any other project, and do not run code: this tests what the
files explain, not what can be executed. If you cannot answer a field, say so; an honest "I could not find this" is
as useful as an answer.

For each field: answer in your own words, as you would explain it to a colleague (a short paragraph); then list the
files you relied on; then say what you could not find, did not understand, or doubt.

## The seven fields

1. **What the system does.** What is this project, what question does it answer, and for whom?
2. **How the data is structured.** What kinds of records are there, how do they relate, and how is something
   unknown represented?
3. **How claims are verified.** How does a statement become something the project shows as established, and who
   decides?
4. **How metrics are produced.** How is a number on a page computed, and how could you check it?
5. **How agents are governed.** What are the project's "agents", who or what acts as them, and what stops one from
   doing something it should not?
6. **How to reproduce the build.** What would you do to rebuild and check everything, and how would you know it
   matched?
7. **Why the product is useful.** Who would find it useful, for what, and what are its main limits? Your own
   judgement is wanted.
