Methodology

ATLAS FRAMING

The brief is generated from the Atlas’s data files and its computed results by a tested build step; nothing on it is typed by hand. Only canonical records appear: records awaiting review are left out. The summary copies values, counts the rows it shows, takes the first and last of a date column, and groups rows; it computes nothing else. A status comes from the Atlas’s status rule on the as-of date. The digest identifies the exact inputs; rebuilding from the same inputs gives the same brief, byte for byte.

DERIVATION supported The term 'HBM' with which rule 2024-28270 names the stacks it adds to ECCN 3A090.c is the alias of the Atlas's record High Bandwidth Memory, and names the same kind of part.

REASONING The rule's sentence names 'HBM stacks'. The Atlas's working definition reads: 'High Bandwidth Memory (HBM) is a memory component: a stack of DRAM dies, placed vertically and connected directly by through-silicon vias.' The rule's word is the record's alias HBM, applied to stacks. 'With a specific memory bandwidth density' limits which stacks the entry covers; that is scope, which stays in the text, not a different class. The rule also defines its term at its edges (p.11: high bandwidth memory includes DRAM ICs whether or not they conform to the JEDEC standards, above its density threshold; co-packaged logic and HBM chips are not covered by 3A090.c), so the text's class and the Atlas's may differ at their edges; the link does not carry either edge. It concludes only that the two words name the same kind of part. It does not conclude that every member of the class, or any product or firm, is controlled, for which destination or under which licence policy, or that the control is in effect on any date (policy-links.md K-2, D-167).

DERIVATION supported The term 'semiconductor manufacturing equipment' that rule 2024-28270 uses for the items it adds new controls on is the name of the Atlas's record Semiconductor manufacturing equipment, and names the same kind of part.

REASONING The rule's summary names 'certain semiconductor manufacturing equipment and related items'. The Atlas's working definition reads: 'semiconductor manufacturing equipment is the class of tools used to fabricate, assemble, test and package chips.' The words are the record's name; 'certain' limits which tools are covered, and 'related items' are not part of the link. It concludes only that the two words name the same kind of part. It does not conclude that every member of the class, or any product or firm, is controlled, for which destination or under which licence policy, or that the control is in effect on any date (policy-links.md K-2, D-167).

designs
The source company performs design work on the target product (a chip or system). Not established by the product's vendor field alone.
fabricates
The source company performs wafer fabrication of the target product's dies (front end, including TSV formation). Not packaging, not design.
incorporates
The source product physically contains the target: a constituent component class, or a named product. The supplier of a class-level part is the edge's supplier qualifier.
operates
The source company runs the day-to-day activity of the target facility. Says nothing about who owns it.
requires
Making or operating any instance of the source class is not possible, at the state of technology the cited source describes, without the target class. Necessity, not current practice.
restricts
The source act's own text names the target class among the items it controls (S18; policy-links.md). Never that every member of the class, or any product or firm, is controlled; never for which destination or under which licence policy; never that the control is in effect today.
supplies
The source company sells or licenses the item (a product or a component class) to the target company, as a claim states at company level.

Analysis, estimates or advice sold by a commercial market-research or consulting firm, including trackers, forecasts and analysts' communications.

A document issued by a standards body through its standards process under a standard designation (e.g. a JEDEC JESD or an ISO/IEC number), defining terms, interfaces or measurement methods.
