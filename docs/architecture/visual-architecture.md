## In short

An Atlas research page answers one question in seven layers, read top to bottom:

`QUESTION → INSIGHT → VISUAL → EXPLORE → EVIDENCE → DATA → METHOD`

- Every layer below the question is **rendered from the same committed SQL result or from canonical
  records**, never typed.
- Every mark says **how the Atlas knows it**: stated by a source, inferred by the Atlas, or a gap
  with its reason in words.
- Every mark opens the evidence behind it.
- A number appears only with its unit and its denominator. A number that is more than a tally of
  the rows a page draws must be a column of a SQL result, with its tutorial.

The design system is deliberately small:

- one ink;
- one tint for classes;
- one amber reserved for gaps;
- three line styles for the three bases;
- a serif for headings and a sans for everything read or counted.

What this document does not do: define metrics (S15), judge them (S16), or build the researcher
product (S20). §11 draws those lines.

## 1. Visual design principles

| # | Principle | What it rules out | From |
|---|---|---|---|
| V-1 | **The question is the entry point.** A page exists to answer one stated question, over a stated population | dashboards of everything; a chart with no question | `CLAUDE.md` §12; MA §19 |
| V-2 | **Finding first, composed from records.** The headline states the answer in record values and fixed words. When the records do not support one sentence, the question is the headline | free-prose headlines; a takeaway that no check can recompute | P-01, P-09; D-105 |
| V-3 | **Fact and interpretation in separate blocks.** A composed finding (values), the Atlas's reading (marked as derived, or as framing), and the Atlas's framing (marked, names no record) never share a sentence | the fused takeaway paragraph (P-23) | `CLAUDE.md` §6 |
| V-4 | **Every number shows its unit and denominator in the same block** ("1 of 4 links"), and says what population it counts: the Atlas's records, never the world | bare counts; shares without a base; "8 links" when four of them repeat one record | P-02, P-12; NG-11 |
| V-5 | **Dates are evidence dates, not ages.** A page shows the earliest and latest evidence date and the input digest. It never says "current" or "updated N days ago": an age needs a clock (`sql-layer.md` §7) | "last updated" badges; relative dates | P-05; DT-S13-4 |
| V-6 | **The basis of every mark is visible, in words, on the mark** | colour-only encodings of evidence; legends as the only key | P-06, P-07, P-11; D-103 |
| V-7 | **An unknown is a mark with a word.** Never a blank, a zero, "none" or a missing row | dropping gaps from a chart; zero-filled cells | `CLAUDE.md` §7; D-104 |

## 2. The research-experience model

`QUESTION → INSIGHT → VISUAL → EXPLORE → EVIDENCE → DATA → METHOD`

Each layer is defined by what the reader gets, where its content comes from, and how a check knows
it is true.

| Layer | The reader gets | Comes from | Checked by |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 QUESTION | the question in plain words, and its scope (which records, which population) | the page specification (an ID list, D-101), the names of records | text provenance (D-105) |
| 2 INSIGHT | a composed finding (headline); a short derived comparison ("in common", "where they differ"); a row of indicator blocks, each with its denominator | the committed SQL result: row values, and tallies of rows | each value recomputed from the result by the checks |
| 3 VISUAL | one primary visual chosen by the grammar (§6), with a reading note | the same SQL result, row by row: one mark per row | each mark's basis and words equal its row |
| 4 EXPLORE | ways to narrow what is shown (by basis, later by layer, accelerator, date) without leaving the page | the same rows; CSS only, no script | the controls are labelled form elements; nothing is hidden when they are unused |
| 5 EVIDENCE | from any mark: the claim, its quoted source sentence, the source, its dates | the canonical evidence view (today the HBM page's panels, `trace.trace_data`) | every evidence link resolves to an existing panel |
| 6 DATA | the result as a table, one row per mark, with every ID behind it | the committed SQL result | every cell equals its result cell (PG-11 pattern) |
| 7 METHOD | the query explained step by step; the definitions; the limits; the input digest; the commands to reproduce | the query file (D-121), decision titles, the build | the tutorial equals the query file; the digest is recomputed |

**Reading order is the hierarchy** (D-116). Plain meaning comes first; IDs, codes and SQL come last
and stay closed until opened. A reader who stops after layer 2 has the answer and its limits. A
reader who goes to layer 7 can rebuild it.

**Progressive disclosure, not progressive truth.** A lower layer may add detail. It may never
contradict, weaken or condition what a higher layer said. Caveats that change the meaning of a
finding belong in the finding's own layer: the indicator block says "1 of 4", not "1" with the
denominator in the method.

## 3. Minimal visual design system

The palette, fonts and line styles of the HBM page (S11, D-103) become named tokens with one job
each. Nothing is added except the type scale and spacing steps, which the page already used
informally. The tokens live in `tools/build_page.py` (`PALETTE`), and both pages import them, so
there is one home for the colours.

### 3.1 Colour roles

| Token | Job | Never used for |
|---|---|---|
| `ink-strong`, `ink-body`, `ink-muted` | text, by hierarchy | marks that carry evidence meaning |
| `ink-link`, `line-focus` | links; the keyboard focus ring | emphasis |
| `line-ink` | stated and inferred lines, node borders, the class rule | — |
| `surface-page`, `surface-panel`, `surface-framing` | background, evidence panels, framing boxes | — |
| `surface-class` | a vendor-neutral class (component, technology): level, not evidence | gaps |
| `surface-gap`, `line-gap`, `ink-gap` | **gaps only**: an unknown value or a recorded gap | warnings, emphasis, "bad" |
| `line-rule` | rules, dividers, the lane, neutral cells | — |

- **Colour is never the only carrier** (D-103, P-15). Every gap also has a word, and the inferred and
  gap lines differ in style.
- **Text never takes a mark's colour,** except the gap ink inside a gap pill, which is a label on
  its own surface.
- **No categorical palette exists yet,** because no visual encodes identity by hue. When one does
  (a map by jurisdiction, a ranking by company), the hues are chosen then, validated for
  colour-vision deficiency, and kept apart from the gap amber.
- Every text colour has at least 4.5:1 contrast against every surface, and every mark colour at
  least 3:1 (WCAG 2.1 AA 1.4.3, 1.4.11). This is checked statically (PG-8, IN-7) and measured in
  the browser.
- **No dark mode** (`vertical-slice.md` §10). It is a candidate for S22, with its own contrast
  checks.

### 3.2 Line and mark vocabulary

| Mark | Meaning | Drawn |
|---|---|---|
| solid line + arrowhead | stated: at least one cited claim is a `FACT` | `line-ink`, 2 px |
| dashed line + arrowhead + the word "inferred" | inferred: every cited claim is the Atlas's `DERIVATION` | `line-ink`, 2 px, dash 6/4 |
| dotted amber line, no arrowhead + a pill with the reason | gap: a recorded gap (a claim the Atlas holds but cannot use) | `line-gap`, dots 2/4 |
| amber pill, dashed border, the state in words | an unknown value (`not researched`, `not publicly determinable`) | `surface-gap` |
| neutral dashed cell, the words "no recorded relationship" | the walk found nothing (RR-7): not a gap the Atlas recorded, but the absence of any record | `line-rule`, no amber |
| box, white | a named thing (company, product, facility) | `line-ink` border |
| box, tinted, thick top rule | a vendor-neutral class | `surface-class` |
| unit square | one row of a result, in an indicator | its basis style, linked to its row |

The difference between **"gap: not researched"** (amber: the Atlas recorded that it does not know)
and **"no recorded relationship"** (neutral: there is no record either way) is the most important
distinction this system draws. It is `CLAUDE.md` §7's "null, unknown, not_applicable and
not_researched are semantically distinct", made visible.

### 3.3 Type

| Role | Face | Size (desktop / phone) |
|---|---|---|
| headline (h1) | the system serif stack | clamp(1.6rem, 3.8vw, 2.4rem) |
| section (h2), sub-section (h3) | serif | 1.45rem; 1.12rem |
| body, labels, tables | the system sans stack | 16 px; tables 0.92rem |
| **indicator values** | **sans**, semibold, proportional figures | 1.7rem (a date range 1.3rem) |
| kicker, keys, table headers on phones | sans, uppercase, letter-spaced | 0.74–0.8rem |
| IDs, digests, SQL | the system monospace stack | 0.86em |

Indicator values use proportional figures (the default); tabular numerals are reserved for aligned numeric columns, which no page has yet. Only system fonts are
used, so nothing is fetched (PG-9).

### 3.4 Space, grid, numbers and words

- **Spacing:** a 4 px base (4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48). Reading width at most 72–80 characters.
  Page width 1120 px, with a 16 px gutter on phones.
- **Breakpoints:** 760 px (tables become labelled cards; chain strips become vertical) and 900 px
  (two-column compositions).
- **Numbers:** integers as digits. Every count has the form "*n* of *d* *unit*". No percentages
  until S15 defines a share and its base. No decimals in tallies.
- **Dates:** ISO 8601 as recorded (`2025-07-22`, or `2020-12` for a partial date); ranges as
  "*from* to *to*". Never a relative date.
- **State words:** the record's own word with `_` shown as a space (`not researched`). Never
  paraphrased, never "unknown", never "none".

## 4. Proposed information architecture for the future Atlas site

The site is organised by **questions**, not by tables (MA §19). This is a proposal for S20, which
builds it; S14.5 builds none of it except the one prototype page.

```text
/                                   What are you trying to understand?  (question cards, P-16)
├── questions/<question>/           one research page per question, in the seven layers (§2)
│     e.g. hbm-insight (prototype), hbm-chain (the S11 journey)
├── dependencies/<component>/       "What depends on X?": the walk from one record (navigate.py)
├── companies/<company>/            a company's links, facilities and identity evidence
├── places/<jurisdiction>/          what is located or incorporated there, and on what evidence
├── evidence/<claim>/               one claim: its statement, citations, quoted sentences, sources
├── sources/<source>/               one source: what it is, when it was read, which claims cite it
├── data/                           every SQL result with its query tutorial and input digest
│     data/<query>/                 (P-17, adapted: evidence dates and digests, not "updated")
└── method/                         the claim model, the source policy, the visual rules (this file)
```

- **One evidence home.** Every page links to `evidence/<claim>/` instead of repeating it. Today the
  HBM page's panels play that role, and the prototype links into them (§12).
- **Question cards** show the question, the composed finding, the evidence-date range and the basis
  mix of the links behind it, as words and unit marks. They never show a thumbnail without words.
- **Profiles** (company, place) are answers to fixed questions ("What does the Atlas record about
  X, and on what evidence?"), so they follow the same seven layers.
- **URLs are IDs.** A page's path is its question's or record's ID, so links survive a rebuild.
  DT-3 (ID stability across commits) must close before any page is published.

## 5. Analytical UX principles

| # | Principle |
|---|---|
| U-1 | **One question, one population, one denominator per page.** A page that needs a second population is a second page |
| U-2 | **The comparison the question implies is made for the reader** (the derived "in common" and "where they differ"), and is recomputed by the checks |
| U-3 | **Exploration narrows; it never re-weights.** Filters show and hide rows; no control changes a measure (P-19 rejected until S15–S16 define one) |
| U-4 | **Every mark is a link, every link is a mark.** A link in the visual leads to evidence; a row in the table leads to the same evidence |
| U-5 | **Scope and limits travel with the finding** into every export, card and indicator (S21) |
| U-6 | **Phone first for reading, desktop first for comparing.** At 375 px every layer reads in one column, with no sideways scroll; desktop aligns small multiples for comparison |
| U-7 | **Keyboard and screen reader are first-class.** Native links, `details` and radio groups only; a visible focus ring; no custom widgets |
| U-8 | **No script unless a question needs it.** The prototype's filter is CSS (`:has()`); without it, everything is shown (graceful degradation) |

## 7. Visual grammar

For each form: the question it answers, when to use it, its encoding, how evidence and unknowns
appear, and when not to use it. "Atlas data today" says whether the canonical records can feed it
now.

### 7.1 Rankings

- **Answers:** which items have more of a measured quantity?
- **Use** a horizontal dot plot or bar, sorted by value, labelled at the end of each mark, on a
  zero-based axis for bars.
- **Evidence:** each mark carries its basis style; a value resting on one publisher carries the
  "one publisher" flag next to its label.
- **Unknowns:** listed below the ranked marks as a separate group, "not researched (n)", **never
  ranked as zero or placed at the end of the axis**.
- **Not when:** fewer than 4 items (state the values), or the items have different units or
  populations.
- **Atlas data today:** no measure exists to rank (NG-11). The first candidate is S15's.

### 7.2 Timelines

- **Answers:** what happened when, and how old is the evidence?
- **Use** a horizontal time axis with event dots (events: announced, completed…) and evidence-date
  bars (from–to) on separate lanes. Partial dates are drawn as their whole period (a month is a
  segment, not a day).
- **Evidence:** each dot opens its event's claims.
- **Unknowns:** an event with no date is listed beside the axis, "undated", never placed at a
  default date.
- **Not when:** there are one or two dates (print them); or ages would need today's date (V-5).
- **Atlas data today:** 4 events and the evidence dates of the S14 result. Possible now; not built,
  because the question does not need it.

### 7.3 Maps

- **Answers:** where is something physically located, or under which jurisdiction?
- **Use** a locator map with points for facilities (place, not headquarters: S09) and shaded
  jurisdictions only for a jurisdiction-level fact.
- **Evidence:** each point opens its `located_in` claim.
- **Unknowns:** a facility with an unknown location is listed beside the map, never placed at its
  owner's headquarters.
- **Not when:** two or three places (a list reads faster); or the question is about dependency, not
  place.
- **Atlas data today:** 2 facilities. A map would decorate (P-24's risk); a list is preferred until
  the facility set grows. A map also needs geometry, whose source must be registered and served
  locally (NG-14).

### 7.4 Dependency diagrams

- **Answers:** what does X need, and through which steps?
- **Use:**
  - **one chain:** a fixed vertical diagram, level by row (the HBM page, D-103);
  - **several chains to compare:** aligned chain strips, one per item, same columns (the
    prototype);
  - **many items:** a matrix (§7.6).
- **Evidence:** line styles per §3.2, every line a link.
- **Unknowns:** gap pills on the line; "no recorded relationship" cells.
- **Not when:** force-directed layouts (position would carry no meaning; D-103), or pictorial chains
  (P-24).
- **Atlas data today:** yes.

### 7.5 Indicator (KPI) blocks

- **Answers:** what is the one thing to remember, as a state, a tally or a range?
- **Use** one label, one value, its denominator and unit, and one line on how it is counted. A block
  may hold:
  - a **state** ("not researched", in gap style);
  - a **tally of drawn rows** ("1 of 4 links", with a unit strip whose squares link to the rows);
  - a **range** ("2018-06-08 to 2026-10-01").
- **Evidence:** the unit strip; each square opens its row.
- **Unknowns:** a state block is the honest form of an unknown indicator; a tally never counts a gap
  as zero, it counts it as a gap.
- **Not when:**
  - the number is a metric no session has defined: it then belongs to S15, as a SQL column with a
    tutorial (§10);
  - one number would hide that rows repeat records. The shared record must be said in the block.
- **Atlas data today:** yes, as tallies of the S14 result's rows (D-124).

### 7.6 Comparison views

- **Answers:** how do several items differ on the same dimensions?
- **Use** small multiples with shared columns and scales (chain strips), or a matrix: items as
  rows, dimensions as columns, cells holding the basis mark and the value in words.
- **Evidence:** each cell is a row of a result.
- **Unknowns:** the cell holds the state word; competing values from different sources are drawn
  side by side, the Atlas's choice marked, none averaged (P-08).
- **Not when:** the items do not share the dimensions; then a list per item.
- **Atlas data today:** 2 accelerators: chain strips (the prototype). A matrix becomes useful from
  about 4 items.

### 7.7 Evidence indicators

- **Answers:** how does the Atlas know this mark?
- **Use** the basis word (stated / inferred / gap), the gap reason, the caveat flags ("one
  publisher", "rests on the Atlas's own definitions", "publisher not linked to a company record"),
  and the evidence date range.
- **Rules:**
  - never a confidence number (none exists, and none may be invented);
  - never a traffic light (status colours imply a verdict);
  - never a count of sources presented as corroboration. Sources listed together do not confirm one
    another (the S14 reading).
- **Atlas data today:** yes (the S14 result's columns).

### 7.8 Analytical tables

- **Answers:** exactly which rows, with which values and IDs?
- **Use** one row per mark of the visual, in the visual's order:
  - the plain columns first (what, how known, when, who published), then the caveats, with the IDs
    in a closed disclosure;
  - at phone width, labelled cards, never a dropped evidence column (P-21);
  - sorting and sparkline columns only when the question needs them (P-20).
- **Rule:** a table is generated from a committed SQL result, and its cells are checked against it
  (PG-11).

## 8. When a visual, when a table, when text

| Situation | Use |
|---|---|
| The answer is a path, a structure or a set of links | a diagram, plus the table |
| The answer is one state or one tally | an indicator block (a chart of one bar is a number) |
| Several items share the same dimensions | aligned small multiples or a matrix, plus the table |
| A quantity across more than 4 items | a ranking or bar, plus the table |
| Change over time with more than 3 dates | a timeline or line, plus the table |
| Fewer than 4 values, or values in different units | text: say the values; do not chart them (the HBM page quotes the two memory capacities and does not compare them) |
| The reader needs IDs, exact strings or every caveat | the table |
| The evidence for one mark | the evidence panel (text: quoted sentences), never a chart |
| A definition, a ruling, a limitation | text |
| The data cannot support the comparison the form implies | no visual; say so in the method layer |

**Test before drawing:** if removing the visual loses nothing the table and one sentence already
say, the visual is decoration (`CLAUDE.md` §15).

## 10. From SQL outputs to visual components

**The binding rule** (D-124):

1. **A visual reads committed inputs only:** a committed SQL result (`sql/results/*.json`) and
   canonical records. It never reads staging, never queries DuckDB at build time (D-118), and never
   holds presentation-only data.
2. **One mark per result row** (or per record, for diagrams drawn from the walk). Every mark carries
   the row number it renders (`data-row`). The checks find the row and compare the mark's basis,
   words and dates with it.
3. **The build renders; it does not compute new facts.** The build may:
   - (a) copy a cell;
   - (b) tally the rows it draws, by a column it shows, over a denominator it prints;
   - (c) take the minimum and maximum of a column it shows;
   - (d) group rows by a key to say what they have in common or where they differ.

   Anything else (a share, a ratio, a concentration, a count of things not drawn) is a **metric**.
   A metric must be a column of a SQL result, defined by S15, with its "How this was computed"
   tutorial (D-115).
4. **Every tally states its unit, its denominator and what repeats.** If the same record appears
   in several rows (the "requires" edge appears under each accelerator), the block says so.
   *(The human decided on 2026-10-06 that the prototype may show such tallies.)*
5. **The checks recompute every derived value independently,** from the result file, without the
   build's code (the WH-10 / PG-11 pattern).

**A visual specification** is the part of a page's build that states, as data:

- its question;
- its result file;
- its row filter, if any;
- its slot or column assignment (in the prototype: position 1 → "acts on it", 2 → "it
  incorporates", 3 → "who makes that part", 4 → "that part requires");
- its encodings (§3.2);
- its denominators;
- its text equivalent.

The build refuses to draw when the rows do not fit the specification, rather than drawing
something misleading. Two examples: two rows in one slot, or a position the specification does not
know.

**What this hands S15:** a metric arrives as a query in `sql/`, with its tutorial and result, and is
shown through §7's grammar, with its denominator in the block. The prototype's tallies are the
presentation S15 can keep, replace with SQL columns, or retire.

## 12. The prototype

**Page:** `site/hbm-insight/index.html` (D-125), generated by `python tools/build_insight.py` from:

- the S14 result `sql/results/accelerator_dependencies.json`;
- the canonical records;
- the query file;
- the HBM page `site/hbm-chain/index.html`: its evidence anchors are the targets of the prototype's
  evidence links, and the build refuses a link with no target.

It is not linked from the HBM page, not published (NG-13), and marked "Prototype" in its kicker. It
reuses `tools/build_page.py`'s machinery (record values, labels, the SQL table and tutorial) by
subclassing it; `build_page.py` and the HBM page are unchanged.

**The seven layers in the prototype:**

1. **Question:**
   - the HBM page's question;
   - the scope line: the number of accelerators in the result, and its evidence dates.
2. **Insight:**
   - the composed headline;
   - "In common" and "Where they differ", derived by position;
   - four indicator blocks: who makes the memory (a state, n of n accelerators); links on stated
     evidence (a tally per accelerator, with unit strips); evidence dated (a range); publishers
     behind a sourced link (a range, listed, not counted as confirmation).
3. **Visual:**
   - one chain strip per accelerator, in aligned columns: what acts on it, the accelerator, what
     it incorporates (with the supplier slot beneath), what that part requires;
   - line styles and words per §3.2;
   - a reading note.
4. **Explore:** a basis filter (all, stated, inferred, gap) that dims the other links in the strips
   and hides the other rows of the table. CSS only.
5. **Evidence:**
   - every link in a strip opens its panel on the HBM page;
   - an evidence index lists each distinct record once, saying how many rows show it.
6. **Data:**
   - the S14 table, with an anchor and a basis attribute on each row;
   - "How this was computed".
7. **Method:**
   - how the page is built;
   - what the counts count;
   - the rulings it depends on;
   - the input digest;
   - the commands to reproduce.

**Checks:** `tests/test_insight.py`, IN-1 to IN-9:

- IN-1: text provenance;
- IN-2: values against their homes and the result;
- IN-3: the derived values recomputed;
- IN-4: the encodings against their rows;
- IN-5: evidence and internal links resolve;
- IN-6: framing;
- IN-7: accessibility basics, palette and contrast;
- IN-8: rebuild byte-identical;
- IN-9: nothing loaded, no script, the prototype marked.
