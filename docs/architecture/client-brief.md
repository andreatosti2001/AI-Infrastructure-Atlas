## In short

- **One brief**, generated by `tools/build_brief.py` from the canonical records and two committed SQL results,
  written to `site/briefs/accelerator-memory/index.html`: a script-free page that prints on A4 and Letter.
- **The question** (H-0): *What does an AI accelerator's memory depend on, who is reported to supply it, and which
  recorded export-control texts name those parts?*
- **It adds no fact.** Every sentence is composed from record values, fixed labels, derived values that a check
  recomputes, or framing that is marked and names no record (D-105). The executive summary is the Client Output's
  one new act: it groups and tallies the dependency result's rows (D-124 rules a to d) and states each group in
  one sentence whose every value a check recomputes.
- **It never advises.** No legal, investment or procurement recommendation; no compliance determination; never
  "a product or firm is controlled" (LA-1 to LA-5, NG-12). A check enforces the forbidden words.
- **Its limits travel with it** (U-5): scope, what is out of scope, the as-of date and the input digest sit in the
  brief's first section, outside any disclosure, and print on its first page; a ready-made citation repeats them
  (P-18).

## 3. Summarising without new facts (the Client Output's act)

The summary is composed by the build from **groups of rows** (D-124 rule d) and **tallies of drawn rows** (rule b).
Each sentence is one list item marked with what it summarises (`data-summary`), so a check can find it and
recompute it. A sentence holds only: result cells (`data-derived="sql-cell"`), record values (`data-ref`), counts
(`data-derived="count"`), statuses (`data-derived="policy-status"`), labels and nothing else.

| Sentence | Composition | The check recomputes |
|---|---|---|
| **depends** | the accelerators that share one `incorporates` row target, each named; the part; what it `requires`; each with its basis cell | exactly the rows with relation `incorporates` and `requires`, grouped by target; every cell equals its result cell |
| **made** | each `designs` or `fabricates` row: "{from} {relation} {to} ({basis})"; a gap row reads "{from} → {to}, {relation}: a recorded gap, not a link ({gap reason})" so that it cannot be read as an assertion | exactly the rows at position 1; a gap row carries its reason and the gap words |
| **supplied** | each supplier row: "{supplier} in {accelerator} ({basis})", with "historical, to {period}" when the row has one and "source-policy criterion not met: {criterion}" for each unmet criterion | exactly the supplier rows; period and criteria shown exactly when the row has them |
| **texts** | the classes the chain reaches, grouped by their state against the acts: named (with each naming act and its status on the as-of date), refused only (with the reasons), no recorded relationship; each group with its count over the number of classes compared | the classes from the result (§3.4); each class's links and candidates from the records; the statuses with `policy_status.derive_status` |
| **limits** | "Acts published after {latest publication date} were not searched." | the latest `published` event date of a recorded act |

**Rules:**
- A group of one is stated in the singular; a group the records leave empty is not stated (no "0 classes").
- A gap is never phrased as a relationship: the gap row of the dependency result reads as a recorded gap with its
  reason, never "TSMC fabricates".
- No adjective that grades (critical, key, major, concentrated, sole); no share, rank or score (NG-11).
- The build refuses to write the brief when the rows do not fit these compositions (for example, a position the
  specification does not know), as the other builds do (D-124 §10).

### 3.4 The classes the chain reaches

The brief compares with the export-control texts every class the dependency result reaches: each accelerator's
class (the `accelerator_class` column: Data-centre GPU, AI ASIC), the class each of those is recorded as narrower
than (`broader`: AI accelerator), and every class at either end of a row (High Bandwidth Memory, 3D die stacking):
**5 classes**. H-0 described 3 (the export page's "dependency journey" classes, D-191): the design adds the two
accelerator classes, because the texts' records refuse both (`cand-015`, `cand-016`, term not matched) and leaving
them out would hide two refusals that concern this chain's accelerators. This is a grouping of record IDs read from
the result and the records (rule d), not a metric. The products themselves (NVIDIA H100, AWS Trainium2) are never
compared: a product meeting a text is a legal determination (LA-3).

## 4. "Recent changes" and "monitoring considerations"

- **Recent changes.** The Atlas runs no change detection (the Change Detector is inactive, D-184), and no act after
  5 December 2024 was searched (D-175). The section therefore shows what is recorded (each dated event of the acts
  shown, in date order; the evidence date range of the chain) and says, in framing, that a change after the latest
  date shown is unknown to the Atlas, not absent. It never says "no changes".
- **Monitoring considerations → "What this brief does not track".** Anything that tells a reader what to watch is
  advice by another name (NG-12, the Boundary). The section is one framing block naming no record: later acts and
  amendments, supplier changes after the evidence dates, accelerators outside the scope, quantities, and whether
  anything falls under a text. It describes the Atlas's coverage, never the reader's next step.

**Print** (measured on the built brief, Chromium 141, 2026-10-14): 65 A4 pages (69 Letter). The brief itself (sections 1
to 9) is 13 pages; the evidence and source appendices start on a new page, in compact type, and fill the rest. The
appendices carry every claim behind the brief (35 panels, 114 claims): printing only the brief's own pages keeps its
scope, limits and citation, and the appendices can be printed when the evidence is needed. A shorter evidence form
is a later UX question (A-4).

**From S22** (D-210, D-214): in print, the evidence appendix's panels give way to `#evidence-print`, every claim the
appendix shows, once each, with its statement, each citation's source and its locator; quoted passages and the hashes
read stay on screen. Measured (Chromium 141, 2026-10-15): **32 A4 pages (33 Letter)**, from 79 (83). The act panels
also show any later official statement about the act that the Change Detector ruled (`change-detection.md` §1), under
"Later statements about this act" (BR-14).

**Copying:** a reader who copies one sentence copies it without its limits; the brief cannot prevent that. The
limits sit in the same section as the summary so that a copied section carries them.

## 8. Breaks (D-177)

New breaks in an S21 list of `tests/regression/breaks.py`, one fault per check (a sentence typed into the template;
a hand-edited value; a count off by one; a mark dropped from the map; a gap phrased as a link in the summary; a
policy cell state changed; the boundary line dropped; a source dropped from the appendix; "should" in framing; a
script; a skipped heading level; a stray page), and controls (a framing sentence reworded; a claim reworded with
everything rebuilt). Breaks that rebuild the results, the HBM page or the journeys also rebuild the brief, which
reads them (as `with_journeys` does for the journeys, D-190). The harness changes, so the full harness runs.

## 9. What a reader of the brief cannot learn from it

- whether any product, firm or transaction is controlled, licensed or exempt;
- anything about accelerators other than NVIDIA H100 and AWS Trainium2, or about the chain beyond memory;
- any act or amendment published after the latest publication date the Atlas holds;
- who supplies HBM today: the namings are reports dated to their evidence;
- quantities: share, price, volume, capacity;
- what to do.
